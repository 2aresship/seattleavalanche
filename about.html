About this newsroom

No newsroom. No staff page. No “our team of veteran journalists.” One person does the entire job: sourcing, verifying, writing, coding, and answering for it.

The short manifesto

Local news died because the money ran out. City hall meetings now happen in empty rooms while decisions that shape decades get made unwatched. This site exists because one person decided an empty room was unacceptable, and because a laptop, public records laws, and stubbornness cost almost nothing.

The Avalanche publishes rarely on purpose. Each story takes weeks: records requests, document cross-checks, conversations verified against evidence. There will never be a feed to doomscroll. There will be work you can trust when it appears.

Why anonymous?

Not for mystique. Anonymity is armor: it keeps pressure, lawsuits, and worse aimed at the work instead of one person’s mailbox. It also removes the incentive to soften coverage of friends, employers, or anyone who might retaliate against a named human being.

Accountability doesn’t require a face. It requires standards. Every claim here must trace to documents or verified sources, corrections are published promptly, and the entire operation can be audited by anyone: the site code itself is downloadable and inspectable.

Non-negotiables

  1. Documents first. Stories run on records.
  2. Corroboration always. Two independent confirmations minimum.
  3. Right of reply. Subjects get real time to respond before publication.
  4. Public corrections. Errors get fixed loudly at the top, never quietly at the bottom.
  5. No ads, no sponsors. Reader-funded only; conflicts can’t be bought.
  6. Collect nothing. No cookies, no trackers, no analytics, no server-side visitor logs.

Questions people actually ask

Are you safe? Are your sources safe?

Source protection here is built into how it works: tips arrive through channels designed so that even this newsroom cannot identify senders, messages are stored encrypted, and identifying details are stripped during verification and never published. If a story couldn’t run without burning someone, it doesn’t run.

How do I know any of this is real?

Trust is earned per-story, and receipts are part of the format: documents are quoted precisely, methods are described, and where legal, source material is published alongside reporting. You are also free to download the whole site, mirror it, and hold these words hostage against revisionism.

Can I republish stories?

Yes. Stories carry a Creative Commons BY license, republish freely with attribution to The Seattle Avalanche and a link. The website code itself is CC0: steal it, fork it, start your own avalanche in your own city. That’s the point.

Why hasn’t Investigation №001 published yet?

Because publishing early burns sources and hands targets time to destroy records. The story goes up when the evidence is nailed down and subjects have had their right of reply. Silence between publications is what diligence looks like from the outside.

I sent a question. When do I get an answer?

Every question is read. Answers go up on the public answers board; if you included a way to reply, you’ll hear back directly. Nothing you sent identifies you unless you chose to sign it.

Hold this newsroom accountable too.

Dispute a fact, demand a correction, or challenge the methods, anonymously if you prefer. Scrutiny makes the work better.
