How freshness works (no stale copies)

Layer 1 · the shell

The HTML/CSS/JS you download rarely changes. When it does, a new ZIP appears on the download page with fresh SHA-256 checksums. Re-download occasionally and re-upload. That’s the entire maintenance burden.

Layer 2 · the living content

Every page loads js/content.js first, then checks for content.remote.json in the site root, whichever is newer wins. Drop an updated avalanche-content.json (renamed to content.remote.json) into your mirror’s root and your mirror instantly serves the newest stories without touching anything else.

Proof of freshness

Every page footer carries a version stamp (e.g. v2026-08-21 14:02). Readers compare stamps across mirrors at a glance; a stale mirror is visible instantly and fixable in one file drop.

The three steps

01

Take the package

Grab the ZIP from the download page. Verify SHA-256 checksums against the manifest there, if they match, you hold exactly what we publish.

02

Host the folder

Any static host works. Fastest options: Netlify Drop (drag the unzipped folder onto app.netlify.com/drop, live in seconds), Cloudflare Pages, or GitHub Pages. No build settings needed, it’s plain HTML.

03

Register your mirror

Send your URL through the source portal. Verified mirrors get added to the public registry below and ship inside future packages, every reader sees your copy listed.

For the paranoid & the dedicated

Tor onion mirror

Run Tor on a small box, point a hidden service at the folder, done, a .onion mirror no registrar can seize. The paper will list verified onion addresses alongside clearnet ones.

IPFS / BitTorrent

The package is just files, pin it with IPFS Desktop or seed the ZIP as a torrent. Content-addressed hosting means tampering breaks the hash chain visibly.

Watchdog duty

If the main domain dies, mirrors are the continuity plan. Bookmark several. If you notice a mirror serving an old version stamp for weeks, nudge its operator or host the newer package yourself.
